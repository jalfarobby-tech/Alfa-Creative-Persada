When Does a Business Need Its Own System, Not Just a Spreadsheet?

October 29, 20264 min read

Many businesses start with a spreadsheet, and that is a sensible choice: quick, cheap, and everyone can use it. The trouble begins when the business grows and the same file is used by too many people for too many purposes.

Signs a spreadsheet is no longer enough

  • There are several versions of the file and nobody is sure which is the latest
  • Data gets changed by people who should only be able to view it
  • Recaps and reports are still compiled by hand every week or month
  • Formulas break when someone moves a column
  • It is hard to trace who changed what and when
  • The file gets slow to open because it is too heavy

If two or three of these feel familiar, you are probably paying a high price in time and input errors.

What your own system gives you

  • Role-based access, so everyone only sees and edits their own part
  • Data kept in one place with a change history
  • Reports and dashboards that update automatically
  • A workflow that follows your business process, such as attendance, approvals, or sales records

It does not have to start big

A common mistake is trying to build everything at once. A safer approach: pick the one process that eats the most time, build a simple version, use it for a few weeks, then grow it based on real use. The spreadsheet does not need to be thrown away either. It often stays useful as an export or backup.

Three questions to start with

  • Which process goes wrong most often or takes the longest?
  • Who needs access to the data, and what are their limits?
  • Which reports are actually used to make decisions?

The answers to these three questions are usually enough to start a discussion. We have built a team management dashboard and an online job application system the same way, starting from one concrete problem.
